**<u>Space Exploration: A Collection of Firsts</u>**

A look at the many famous “firsts” throughout the history of spaceflight, and the brave pioneers who inspired the world.

**<u>First artificial satellite</u>** – The Soviet Union launched **Sputnik 1** into orbit in **1957**. It circled the earth taking temperature and pressure readings, as well as beeping. This caused a panic in other parts of the world, particularly the United States, and can be seen as the flashpoint of the entire Space Race.

**<u>First animal in orbit</u>** – The first animal to orbit was the dog **Laika.** She was launched by the Soviets in **1957**. Unfortunately, she did not survive the flight.

**<u>First American artificial satellite</u>** – The Americans succeeded in placing a satellite into orbit in **1958**. It was called **Explorer 1**. The American satellite confirmed the existence of the **Van Allen radiation belts** around the Earth and measured impacts from cosmic dust.

**<u>First monkeys to survive spaceflight</u>** – The American “Monkeynauts”, **Able and Baker**, were the first primates to survive a trip to space. They were launched aboard a Jupiter rocket in **1959**. During their 16-minute flight, they withstood 38 Gs of force (almost 13 times the force of the G-Force Accelerator!)

**<u>First man in space</u>** – The first man in space was the Soviet cosmonaut, **Yuri Gagarin.** He flew his mission in **April of 1961**, spending over an hour and a half in space and returning safely to the Earth. **Gagarin** became a hero in the USSR, and the success of the Soviets further pushed the United States to focus on space exploration.

**<u>First man to Orbit the Earth</u>** – Soviet cosmonaut **Yuri Gagarin** has this one to his name too. On that famous first flight, he actually did complete one orbit of the Earth before safely landing. The Soviet Union got the 2 for 1 deal with his flight, achieving two major milestones with one launch.

**<u>First American in Space</u>** – American astronaut **Alan Shepard’s** first flight was actually scheduled for 1960, but was delayed several times until **May 5, 1961**, almost a month after **Gagarin’s** first flight. Although it was disappointing to miss out on being the first man in space, Shepard’s flight was successful. The **Mercury Redstone** rocket carried him up into space and right back down – total flight time of about 15 minutes.

**<u>First American to orbit the Earth</u>** – American astronaut **John Glenn** became the first American to orbit the Earth in **1962**. After **Alan Shepard’s** first flight**, Gus Grissom** performed largely the same mission. NASA, already behind in the space race, changed tactics and used the more powerful **Mercury Atlas** rocket to launch **Glenn** into orbit. He orbited the Earth 3 times, spending almost 5 hours total in space. Glenn would not return to space until 1998, when he flew aboard the **Space Shuttle *Discovery*** at the age of 77. He remains the only person to fly in both the **Mercury** and **Space Shuttle** programs.

**<u>First woman in space</u>** – The first woman in space was the Soviet woman named **Valentina Tereshkova**. A textile worker and amateur parachutist turned cosmonaut, she went to space in **1963**, and remains the only woman to ever perform a solo space mission. She orbited the earth 48 times, spending almost 3 full days in space – a longer time than all six **Mercury** astronauts combined!

**<u>First Extravehicular Activity (EVA)</u>** – The first **EVA** was performed by cosmonaut **Alexei Leonov**. He performed this first spacewalk in **1965** during the **Voskhod 2** Mission. **Leonov’s** spacewalk lasted around 12 minutes, and was almost disastrous, as his spacesuit had overinflated, making it difficult to re-enter the capsule. **Leonov** also oversaw the training of new cosmonauts and commanded the **Soyuz** portion of the **Apollo-Soyuz test project**, the first joint mission between the US and the USSR. Fun Fact – it is believed that the Soviets intended **Leonov** to be the first cosmonaut on the moon. However, the Soviet lunar missions were cut short after the failures of their **N-1** moon rocket.

**<u>First American EVA</u>** – The first American to perform an **EVA** was astronaut **Ed White.** He performed America’s first spacewalk during the Gemini 4 mission in **1965**, six months after the Soviets performed the first ever **EVA**. He was out of the spacecraft for around 20 minutes and loved every minute of it. **NASA** actually had to order **White** to return to the spacecraft, which **White** described as “the saddest moment of my life”. **Ed White** would tragically be killed in the **Apollo 1** fire which also claimed the lives of fellow astronauts **Gus Grissom** and **Roger Chaffee**.

**<u>First Rendezvous of Spacecraft</u>** – Although it may be argued by some that the Soviet Union achieved **Rendezvous** of spacecraft first, they were only able to launch their spacecraft into similar orbits. The two craft would come within a few kilometers of each other, but their orbital paths quickly diverged, carrying them thousands of kilometers away from each other. The first true rendezvous of spacecraft occurred between **Gemini 7** and **Gemini 6A** in late **1965**. **Wally Schirra and Tom Stafford (6A)** met up in orbit with **Frank Borman and Jim Lovell (7).** The two spacecraft were able to hold station only about a foot apart from each other. If they had been equipped to dock, they could have. There was a period of about 20 minutes where the two craft maintained their position without requiring any course corrections at all! Now that’s **rendezvous**!

**<u>First Docking of Spacecraft</u>** – The next logical step after rendezvous is docking. The first docking of two spacecraft occurred in **1966**, on the **Gemini 8** mission. It would be **Neil Armstrong**, later to be the first man on the moon, who actually performed the first successful docking when he docked with the **Agena Target Satellite** (Safer than trying to dock two manned spacecraft). Although the docking was successful, the mission took a turn for the worse when the two docked craft began an uncontrolled tumble spin. Fortunately, **Armstrong** was able to regain control of his capsule, saving the lives of himself and his crewmate, **David Scott**, as well as probably saving the entire space program!

**<u>First manned mission to orbit moon</u>** – The first manned mission to orbit the moon was **Apollo 8**, in **December of 1968**. The crew – **Frank Borman, Jim Lovell, and William Anders** – became the first three humans in history to leave the Earth’s orbit and achieve orbit around the moon. This means they were also the first to see the far side of the moon, as well as the first people to get a view of the entire sphere of the Earth! Their famous trip around the moon and back proved that humans could achieve something that had long been only a dream.

**<u>First man on the moon</u>** – The first man on the moon was American astronaut **Neil Armstrong**, who first set foot upon the moon on **July 21, 1969. Armstrong**, along with **Edwin “Buzz” Aldrin,** his **Lunar Module Pilot (LMP)** became the first two moonwalkers during the **Apollo 11** mission. Eventually, **12** total men would walk on the moon.

**<u>First space station</u>** – The first space station was the Soviet space station named **Salyut**. After the failure of their lunar program, the Soviets decided to turn their attention closer to home, focusing on long-term stays in low-Earth orbit. **Salyut** was launched in **1971**, hosting one crew for a period of 24 days (a spaceflight record at the time).

**<u>First American space station</u>** – After the conclusion of the **Apollo** program, which ended in **1972** after the **Apollo 17** lunar mission, the Americans also turned their attention to low-Earth orbit. The first American space station was **Skylab**. **Skylab** was launched in **1973** aboard a **Saturn V rocket**, the last ever use of the **Saturn V**. **Skylab** would host **three** crews, each of which stayed longer than the last, the **third** crew setting the new record of **84** days in space. After the three missions, **Skylab** remained unoccupied, finally reentering the Earth’s atmosphere in **1979**.

**<u>First American woman in space</u>** – The first American woman in space was **Sally Ride.** Late to the party, the Americans did not send a woman into space until **1983.** **Sally Ride** would fly twice, both times on the **Space Shuttle *Challenger.*** While training for her third flight in **1986**, the ***Challenger*** exploded, causing a halt in the Space Shuttle program. Ride retired from NASA in **1987**.
